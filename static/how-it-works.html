How it works

Plain words, no magic.

This page says exactly what Heard does today, what the calibration reading is for, and what we haven’t built yet. If a claim isn’t here, we’re not making it.

1 · Your phrases, in your voice

In the app, you save the things you actually say — “Water, please”, “I need the bathroom”, or your own words like wawa — and record each one a few times. Heard checks everything you say against those recordings.

2 · Two ways of listening

When you hold the talk button and speak, Heard does two things at once. It writes out the words it heard, using a speech model that runs on the device itself (nothing is sent to a cloud service). And it compares the shape of the sound against your saved recordings.

Usually the written-out words lead, so you can say anything, not just what you’ve saved. When the words match one of your phrases, you get that phrase with its picture. When the sound clearly matches a personal word like wawa, that wins, because a general speech model can spell “wawa” but can’t know it means water. Either way, the other good guesses are one tap away, and choosing none is always allowed.

Honestly: the speech model is general-purpose, the same kind of technology that often gets atypical speech wrong. It will sometimes mishear you. That is why nothing is said until you confirm it.

3 · Nothing is said without you

A phrase is only spoken aloud — and can only be sent to anyone — after you confirm it. “Yes” and “No” are two large buttons, every answer can be undone for a few seconds, and a wrong guess from us never costs you anything but a tap.

4 · The calibration reading

Calibration asks you to read ten short sentences that together contain every sound of English. Each one is shown in full: press “Start recording”, say it in your own way and at your own pace, then “I’ve read it”. Nothing follows along word by word or checks whether you got a word “right” — it never marks you wrong, because you can’t be: it’s your voice. Afterwards the server cuts the take into one clip per word, using the speech recognizer’s timings.

Honestly: today those recordings are stored by Heard on the computer it runs on, as per-word clips, and nothing more. They’re the raw material for the next step — learning how you produce each sound, so Heard can understand words you never recorded. That part is being built; it is not live.

What we don’t do

  • No account, no cloud profile. Audio stays on the device running Heard.
  • No silent corrections — Heard never “fixes” your speech, it learns it.
  • No scores, streaks, or accuracy grades. You are not a dataset with feelings; you’re a person we work for.

Under the hood

  • TranscriptionWhisper (base.en via faster-whisper) on the local CPU, nudged toward your saved phrases; silence and Whisper’s stock hallucinations are filtered out.
  • MatchingMFCC features compared with dynamic time warping against your sample recordings; confidence needs a clear margin before anything is called certain.
  • SpeakingA natural synthesized voice when a key is configured, with the browser’s built-in voice as the fallback.
  • CalibrationEach sentence is recorded whole, then cut into per-word clips on the server from Whisper’s word timestamps; every clip keeps the word that was asked for alongside what was heard.
  • StorageJSON and audio files on disk, on the computer running Heard — for the app and for calibration recordings alike. No cloud storage, no account.
  • PlannedA per-person phonetic profile learned from calibration and everyday yes/no taps. In progress, not shipped.
