About

The software is wrong.
Never the person.

Heard started with a simple observation at a hackathon table: the people who would benefit most from voice technology are the people it serves worst. Dictation that types nonsense. Assistants that answer a question nobody asked. The quiet arithmetic of deciding it’s easier not to speak.

Every design decision in Heard follows from one rule: when recognition fails, that is the software’s failure. So the app never says “try again”, never scores anyone’s speech, and never acts on a guess without a yes. The reading screen can’t show an error, because we didn’t build one.

The rules we hold ourselves to

  • Honesty about capability. What works is on the how-it-works page; what doesn’t work yet is labeled as such, everywhere, every time.
  • The person sets the pace. No time limits, no auto-anything that can’t be overridden, targets big enough for unsteady hands.
  • Voice data is personal. Recordings stay on the device. No account, no upload, no quiet telemetry.

Who we are

A small student team building Heard at HackPrinceton 2026. We’re not speech-language pathologists, and we’d rather borrow the expertise of people who are — if that’s you, or if Heard is for someone you love, we genuinely want to hear what we got wrong.

This is a prototype built in a weekend. It will have rough edges; it won’t have dishonest ones.
