The problem

Speech recognition was built on newsreader voices. Millions of people don’t sound like that — and never will.

Cerebral palsy, a stroke, Down syndrome, ALS, stuttering — any of these can put a voice outside what the big systems were trained on. The result is familiar to anyone who lives it: you repeat yourself three times, and the assistant plays the wrong song anyway.

Our position is simple: if Siri already understands you, you don’t need us. Heard is for everyone else.

Where we are

● Working today

  • Your phrase book. Save the things you say often — including your own words, like “wawa” for water — with a few recordings each.
  • Talk mode. Hold a button and speak. Heard writes out what it heard, using a speech model that runs on the device itself, and checks it against your saved recordings, so personal words like “wawa” still come through.
  • You confirm first. Unsure results come back as three big choices. Nothing is spoken for you until you tap yes.
  • A clear voice out loud. Confirmed phrases are spoken aloud for the person you’re talking to.

Where we’re headed

  • Learning your sounds, not just your phrases. The calibration reading on this site collects the material for that — today it records and stores; the learning isn’t live yet.
  • New words, in your voice. Today, anything you haven’t recorded goes through a general-purpose speech model, the same kind that often struggles with atypical speech. Learning how you produce each sound is how we plan to close that gap. We’re not there yet, and we won’t pretend otherwise.
  • Getting better as you use it. Every yes-or-no tap is honest training data for the versions ahead.

How it works

  1. Read a short passage

    Ten small sentences, about three minutes, written to carry every sound in English. You can pause, skip, or re-do any of them.

  2. Say what you need

    In the app, hold the button and speak. Heard writes out the words it heard and shows them back to you, with your closest saved phrases one tap away.

  3. Confirm, and be heard

    Tap yes and your phrase is spoken aloud, clearly. Tap no and you choose the right one — which quietly makes next time better.

Three minutes of reading. A voice that’s yours.
